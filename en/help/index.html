How to use the Nomad360 business calculator

Nomad360 lite is a free business calculator that runs in your browser. Enter your own idea and your own numbers. It works out the cost of a product or service, the price you need, advertising cost per customer, taxes, loan or own-funds needs and monthly cash flow.

It does not find buyers. Once you have a price, you still need to check who will pay it. See H17.

Open the calculator

Quick start

  1. Open the calculator. Under Which currency will we use to calculate the business?, pick your currency.
  2. Under What do you sell?, click Product, A service or I already sell several different things.
  3. Fill in the steps in order. A field that needs fixing is highlighted.
  4. Read the result. Change one number at a time to see what moves.
  5. Save it in the browser, or export a PDF (H10, H11).

Want to look first? Open View a sample calculation for a cotton tote bag on the home screen. It is a demo with filled-in figures, and it does not replace your own calculation.

Which route? Go to H01. Running a consultancy site? Go to H12.

H01. How do I choose between V1 and V2?

V1 calculates one product or one service at a time. V2 puts several products and services into one portfolio and pays shared costs once.

You want to…ClickYou get
Price one productProductV1 product flow: cost, production, logistics, sales expenses, advertising, price, cash flow.
Price one serviceA serviceV1 service flow. Choose Simplified calculation or Full calculation.
Run several items that share rent, staff or a warehouse, starting from your V1 resultsOpen V2 portfolio economics → at the end of a V1 resultLinked V2: V1 costs, forecasts, price bounds and taxes carry over. You add shared resources.
Run a whole store or portfolio from scratchI already sell several different thingsStandalone V2, titled “My products and real cash”. It has its own draft and does not read V1.

In V1, a product, a service and a simplified or full service calculation are different routes. None of them is the same thing as V2.

If you are calculating a product in V1

  1. On the home screen select Product, enter its name and start calculating. At the sourcing step choose I make it myself, I buy from others, or Dropshipping. These are different cost and cash-timing models, not three labels for the same calculation.
  2. Own production: begin with materials and production, then manufacturing expenses, sellable units after defects, logistics and selling costs. Resale: enter the purchase of finished goods in batches and the subsequent cost of selling them. Dropshipping: enter supplier fulfillment, shipping and settlement timing; do not invent your own production or warehouse if you have neither.
  3. After direct costs, enter selling expenses, advertising assumptions, taxes, target margin and discounts. Read not just the customer price but also the V1 price range, cash flow and funding requirement. H02 explains the product input stages in detail.

If you are calculating a service in V1

  1. Select A service. Under the calculation-depth choice, select Simplified calculation or Full calculation. These are two ways of modelling one service; neither is the multi-item V2 portfolio.
  2. Choose Online or Offline. For an online service, indicate who provides it: I do it myself, I hire someone else, or I am an agent. Each path has distinct costs and revenue ownership, so fill only the fields that appear for your selection.
  3. For an offline service, account for consumables, premises, labour and maximum services per month: demand cannot exceed actual capacity without adding resources. For an online service, also inspect the marketing funnel and who delivers the work. Available fields depend on the selected mode.
  4. If you act as an agent, separate the customer's payment to the principal from your commission income and specify who pays each expense. The custom incentive scheme choice is not an automatic calculation engine for arbitrary commission contracts. See H02 and H08.

Two distinct ways to use V2

Linked V2 starts from calculated V1 items, carrying over their inputs, price limits and applicable terms. You then add shared resources such as rent, employees, a warehouse or campaigns once for the portfolio. Do not count costs already included in V1 again. See H05.

Standalone V2 opens from I already sell several different things on the home screen. Build the assortment from scratch: items, sales-unit mix, common advertising, resources and funding. It does not import the V1 draft. Multiple goods in one V1 order are also not the same as a portfolio with shared business costs.

What to do after choosing a route

First calculate direct costs and the price required by your economics; then assess company-wide expenses and cash separately. Only then ask: who will buy from you, why, and where, at that calculated price? Which value, evidence and product presentation would persuade them? Test those assumptions with buyers and recalculate. The calculator does not verify demand (H17).

H03. How do I set a selling price and target margin?

You do not type a price. You enter your costs, taxes, financing and the margin you want, and the calculator gives the price that delivers it.

In V1

  1. Finish the cost sections, advertising, Portfolio VAT and Business tax (On revenue or On profit, plus Tax rate, %). If no business tax is selected, you will see: “Select revenue tax or profit tax above to calculate a numeric price.”
  2. Enter your target margin. Under Will you offer discounts? choose Yes or No, then set Maximum discount, % and Minimum margin, %. The minimum cannot exceed the target.
  3. Read the price block, titled “final price including loan and taxes”: Product price, VAT and Final customer price.
  4. Read the Price-list range. The lower end keeps your minimum net margin after the maximum discount, commissions, taxes and allocated business costs. The upper end keeps your target net margin.

If the calculator reports that no profitable price is possible at these tax, commission and margin rates, reduce costs or lower the target margin.

Margin is not markup

Example (illustrative numbers)

Cost 100, price 125 before tax.

Margin = (125 − 100) ÷ 125 = 20%. Markup = (125 − 100) ÷ 100 = 25%.

The calculator's margin fields are margins on price. Markup divides by cost.

In V2

  • Standalone V2: each product has Target product margin, %. The base price comes from that target and the base CAC. The actual margin after weighted CAC can differ, and the result shows Weighted product profit margin, %.
  • Linked V2: the V1 price range stays fixed. V2 shows the Calculated V2 list price, which cannot exceed the approved V1 ceiling. The status text tells you whether the target or minimum margin is reached.

A product's margin before shared costs is not the profit of the whole business. See H05 and H06. More: product and service pricing calculator.

H04. What do CAC, CPC, CTR, conversions and ROAS mean?

In the Advertising expenses section (V1) or 1. Advertising and marketing (standalone V2) you enter a plan, and the calculator turns it into forecast customers. Nothing here is measured. It follows from your inputs.

FieldMeans
Monthly advertising budgetWhat you plan to spend on ads per month.
Average cost per click (CPC)Budget ÷ CPC = clicks.
CTR — ad click-through rate, %Clicks as a share of impressions. Used to show impressions.
Conversion: click → lead / inquiry, %Share of clicks that become leads.
Conversion: lead / inquiry → purchase, %Share of leads that buy.
Marketing manager fee / monthEnter 0 if there is none.

Example (illustrative numbers)

  • Budget 1,000, CPC 2 → 500 clicks.
  • 10% click → lead: 50 leads.
  • 20% lead → purchase: 10 purchases.
  • Base CAC = 1,000 ÷ 10 = 100.
  • With 5% CTR, 500 clicks mean 10,000 impressions.

What you see. V1 shows impressions, clicks, leads, purchases, units sold, Net advertising budget per unit, Marketing fee per unit and Total customer acquisition cost per unit. Base CAC is the advertising budget ÷ purchases. The marketing fee is shown separately.

Standalone V2 adds a CAC buffer ratio, % (100% means no buffer; 75% means CAC ÷ 0.75), a Minimum period, months, and a weighted CAC that spreads the shared budget across products. The result also shows Business ROAS, as revenue ÷ ad spend.

V1 service results show CPA / CAC and ROMI. CPA covers advertising. CAC includes advertising, delivery, processing, overhead and amortization. Turnover tax is excluded. In V1 the CAC is the cost of winning a customer and serving them, so it is larger than CPA.

Advertising cost is part of cost, so these inputs can change the price you need. A forecast funnel is not proof of demand (H17).

H05. How are shared expenses allocated in V2?

A shared expense is one payment that serves several products, such as rent, a team or a certificate. Enter it once, then say which products it serves.

Standalone V2 — nine sections

  1. Advertising and marketing: one portfolio funnel (H04).
  2. Products and sales mix: for each product choose I make it, I buy it from others or Dropshipping, then enter its cost, Target product margin, % and Forecast sales mix by UNITS, %. All products together must equal 100%. Use + Add product.
  3. Procurement and certificates.
  4. Production: premises, staff, equipment.
  5. Warehouse and logistics.
  6. Sales and team.
  7. Advertising budgets and marketing staff: add only separate campaigns and fees. The shared budget already follows each product's revenue share.
  8. Other business expenses: website, domain, content and so on.
  9. Taxes, owner funds and credit (H07, H08).

Adding a shared resource

In standalone V2, use sections 3 to 8. In linked V2, use 2. Shared resources and click + Add shared resource.

  1. Pick the Resource type: Premises, Workers, Warehouse rent, Equipment, Certificate / license, Sales staff, Advertising management, Separate advertising campaign, Website, Hosting, Domain, Content or Other shared expenses.
  2. Enter Amount and set Payment to Monthly or One time.
  3. Under Resource supports products, select every product it serves. One payment is shared by several products. Do not charge it twice.
  4. Under How to allocate across products, choose By sales revenue or By actual utilization. For utilization, enter each product's usage in one unit (hours, days or a proportion) and, if there is a limit, the Total resource capacity.
  5. A certificate can cover several products only if its country, product scope and validity cover all of them.

Example (illustrative numbers)

Rent of 300 a month serves two products. The business pays 300, not 600. The shares given to the two products must add up to 300.

In linked V2, if a cost is already inside a V1 card, mark it Already in SKU cost (or the matching “Already in SKU…” option) and enter the original amounts. They are replaced by one actual payment. Otherwise the cost is counted twice.

The result table has an Allocated to column. It shows which products each shared cost was assigned to. Warehouse costs cannot go to offline services, because services are not stocked.

H06. Why is profit different from cash flow and Available Cash?

Profit says whether sales earned more than they cost. Cash flow says when money moves. A profitable business can still run out of money.

Example (illustrative numbers)

Month 1: you pay a supplier 600 for 10 units. Cash: −600.

Month 2: you sell 2 units at 100, before tax. Revenue 200. Cost of goods sold 2 × 60 = 120. Gross profit 80.

Cash after month 2: −600 + 200 = −400. Profit is positive. You are still 400 short.

Where to look in the result.

  • AVAILABLE CASH is the cumulative cash balance, including owner funding or a loan, minus the protected reserve. It is not profit and it is not free cash flow in the accounting sense. A negative number is a cash deficit.
  • The monthly table (standalone V2) has: Month, Sales, Payments, Operating cash flow, Loan / owner, Interest, Principal repayment, Net cash flow and Cumulative cash flow.
  • Loan principal repaid is a cash outflow, not an operating expense. Owner funding and a loan received are financing, not profit.
  • Purchases of stock are a cash advance. Cost of goods sold is recognised when you sell, so the purchase is not counted twice.
  • A month is 30 days.
  • In standalone V2, the base-month net profit is after business tax and before loan interest. EBITDA · baseline month is shown separately.

H07. How do I compare my own funds with a business loan?

The calculator works out the money you need before sales cover your costs, including a reserve. You then choose how to fund it.

  • Standalone V2, section 9: Capital source = Own funds or Loan. For a loan enter Annual credit rate, % and Loan term, months. Set Protected reserve, % of cash deficit.
  • V1 products: use Calculate a loan (Loan principal, Annual loan rate, %, Loan term, months). It shows monthly interest, total interest and the total amount repayable. Set Reserve, % of planned expenses.
  • V1 full service: Funding = Own funds or Loan, with Reserve, Annual loan rate, % and Loan term (months). The results include Startup capital, Peak deficit, First positive CF, Payback and Outstanding debt.

How the loan works. You pay interest every month and repay the whole principal in the final month (a bullet loan, not an annuity). The loan term can be longer than the sales period. A longer term adds interest, not sales. The cash schedule continues until repayment.

Example (illustrative numbers)

Loan 1,200 at 12% a year for 6 months. Interest each month = 1,200 × 12% ÷ 12 = 12. Total interest 72. The 1,200 is repaid in month 6.

The calculator reminds you that the loan rate should be below your margin. Compare the two scenarios by running the demo cotton tote with No loan · 5 months and With loan · 6 months (sample).

H08. How do I choose between VAT, Sales Tax and no sales tax?

Two separate settings:

  1. Tax mode (tax on sales): VAT / IVA, Sales tax or No sales tax. For VAT you enter the rate. For Sales Tax you enter Sales Tax rate, %.
  2. Business tax: On revenue or On profit, with Tax rate, %. VAT is separate and not an alternative to this tax.

You type every rate yourself. The calculator does not know your country's rates.

Sales Tax is added to the price after the discount. It passes through to the state and is not your revenue. The V2 results show revenue without tax, the Sales Tax total and the total the buyers pay.

Example (illustrative numbers)

Price 100, discount 10%, Sales Tax 8%. Price after discount 90. Sales Tax 90 × 8% = 7.20. The buyer pays 97.20. Your revenue is 90.

A notice appears next to the tax fields: individual tax deductions and reliefs are not included, and an accountant should advise on optimizing taxes. Payroll rates are reference values. Confirm them with your accountant.

Agent and principal taxes are modelled monthly, not on actual filing dates.

H09. How can I convert a foreign-currency purchase?

Your business currency is the one you picked on the home screen. A purchase or a carrier's invoice can be in another currency.

  1. Next to the amount, choose the payment currency. It defaults to your business currency. Choosing it does not change the amount you typed or apply a rate.
  2. In the Currency converter, click Get reference rate. The calculator fetches the Exchange rate, the Rate date and the Rate source. Daily reference rates come from Frankfurter. Your bank's rate may differ.
  3. Or type a Manual rate, with a date and a source. This is also what to do if you see “The reference rate is unavailable.”
  4. Click Apply to calculation. Until you do, the payment is marked “Apply a rate to use this payment in the calculation.”

Example (illustrative numbers)

A purchase of 1,000 CNY at a rate of 0.14 USD per CNY is 140 USD in a USD model.

No bank fee or spread is added. Enter it as a cost if you pay it. Each payment is converted once. A saved calculation keeps its own rate and does not update when the market moves. A saved calculation also opens in its own currency.

PDF

  1. Finish pricing, tax and cash flow. Otherwise you will see “Complete pricing, tax and Cash Flow before saving the PDF.”
  2. Click Export to PDF (V1) or Save calculation as PDF (V1 full service, V2).
  3. Your browser's print dialog opens. Choose Save as PDF. No data is uploaded.

Excel price list (V2)

  2. Open Create a price list? and click Create price list.
  3. Click Download Excel price list (or Save price list as PDF).
  • It is a real .xlsx file, made in your browser. Prices are numbers, and a Notes sheet holds the warnings.

  • If you change the calculation or the language, you will see “The calculation or language changed. Download the price list again.”

H12. How can I add a ready-made business calculator to my consultancy website?

Nomad360 lite is a finished financial calculator for small-business clients: costs, price, margin, taxes and cash needs. You put it on your own site as an HTML file.

  1. Open the calculator and scroll to the bottom. In the section for businesses, click Add the calculator to your website. A comparison table, Calculator editions, opens.
  2. Click Download the free version. The page shows “Preparing HTML…”, then “HTML download ready”. If nothing downloads, use the click here link next to “If the download did not start”.
  3. Publish the file on your site. In your site editor, add it as a custom HTML block with JavaScript enabled. Or give it a page of its own. What works depends on your platform.
  4. Test it as a visitor: the calculator starts, styles and links work, downloads work and it is usable on a phone.

Automatic updates are not supported. Download the file again to get a newer version.

The free edition is provided “as is”. You may embed the complete, unmodified edition on a commercial website, keeping its branding, attribution, links, contacts and developer-support information. Modifications and rebranding need a separate written agreement. See Licence and use and H14.

H13. How can the calculator help visitors prepare for a consultation?

A visitor works through the calculator alone, before they contact you: product cost, the price they need, advertising, taxes, funding and monthly cash flow. They arrive with their own numbers and with concrete questions, so the conversation can start from specifics.

Questions a visitor can take to a specialist after a calculation:

  1. Is any cost missing from my list?
  2. The calculator gives this price. How does it compare with alternatives?
  3. Who would buy this from me at this price, and how do I check?
  4. Which shared costs might I be underestimating?
  5. How much money do I need before the first sales, and how should I fund it?
  6. Which tax set-up should I use?
  7. What should I test first in a small pilot?

The calculator does not qualify leads and does not send results to you. The visitor brings the numbers.

H16. Which SEO topics belong on my site with the calculator?

A calculator is interactive, so a search engine reads the text on the page around it. You control that text, the page title and description, and internal links. Put the calculator on a page that answers a real question your clients ask, and write that answer in plain text:

  • how to calculate the cost of a product or service,
  • how to set a selling price and target margin,
  • what a business needs to start: costs, funding, reserve,
  • how to read a small business cash flow,
  • VAT, Sales Tax and business tax, as your local rules apply.

One question, one page. Do not create several pages competing for the same search phrase.

Nomad360 does not promise rankings, traffic or leads. With White Label, search adaptation is built around the niche, region and queries you agree on, not a generic keyword list.

H17. What does this calculation not verify?

It checks the economics of the numbers you entered. It does not check anything outside them.

  • Demand and prices in the market. The advertising funnel is your forecast. It does not find buyers, show why they would buy at this price, or design your offer and packaging.
  • Taxes. It does not choose your rates, apply individual deductions or reliefs, or prepare returns.
  • Legal and licensing requirements for your business.
  • Payroll rates. The reference rates are indicative. Check them with an accountant.
  • Your inputs. Prices, conversions and costs are only as good as what you enter.
  • Profit. A calculated result is not a guarantee.

How to test it: get supplier quotes, talk to the people you expect to buy, run a small pilot, and review your numbers with an accountant. Then change your inputs and recalculate. See also business idea feasibility calculator.
