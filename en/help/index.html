How to use the Nomad360 business calculator

Nomad360 lite is a free business calculator that runs in your browser. Enter your own idea and your own numbers. It works out the cost of a product or service, the price you need, advertising cost per customer, taxes, loan or own-funds needs and monthly cash flow.

It does not find buyers. Once you have a price, you still need to check who will pay it. See H17.

Open the calculator

Quick start

  1. Open the calculator. Under Which currency will we use to calculate the business?, pick your currency.
  2. Under What do you sell?, click Product, A service or I already sell several different things.
  3. Fill in the steps in order. A field that needs fixing is highlighted.
  4. Read the result. Change one number at a time to see what moves.
  5. Save it in the browser, or export a PDF (H10, H11).

Want to look first? Open View a sample calculation for a cotton tote bag on the home screen. It is a demo with filled-in figures, and it does not replace your own calculation.

Which route? Go to H01. Running a consultancy site? Go to H12.

H01. How do I choose between V1 and V2?

V1 calculates one product or one service at a time. V2 puts several products and services into one portfolio and pays shared costs once.

You want to…ClickYou get
Price one productProductV1 product flow: cost, production, logistics, sales expenses, advertising, price, cash flow.
Price one serviceA serviceV1 service flow. Choose Simplified calculation or Full calculation.
Run several items that share rent, staff or a warehouse, starting from your V1 resultsOpen V2 portfolio economics → at the end of a V1 resultLinked V2: V1 costs, forecasts, price bounds and taxes carry over. You add shared resources.
Run a whole store or portfolio from scratchI already sell several different thingsStandalone V2, titled “My products and real cash”. It has its own draft and does not read V1.

In V1, a product, a service and a simplified or full service calculation are different routes. None of them is the same thing as V2.

If you are calculating a product in V1

  1. On the home screen select Product, enter its name and start calculating. At the sourcing step choose I make it, I buy it from others, or Dropshipping. These are different cost and cash-timing models, not three labels for the same calculation.
  2. Own production: begin with materials and production, then manufacturing expenses, sellable units after defects, logistics and selling costs. Resale: enter the purchase of finished goods in batches and the subsequent cost of selling them. Dropshipping: enter supplier fulfillment, shipping and settlement timing; do not invent your own production or warehouse if you have neither.
  3. After direct costs, enter selling expenses, advertising assumptions, taxes, target margin and discounts. Read not just the customer price but also the V1 price range, cash flow and funding requirement. H02 explains the product input stages in detail.

If you are calculating a service in V1

  1. Select A service. Under the calculation-depth choice, select Simplified calculation or Full calculation. These are two ways of modelling one service; neither is the multi-item V2 portfolio.
  2. Choose Online or Offline. In Simplified calculation for an offline service, the expense form opens directly without a separate provider choice. An online service, or either channel under Full calculation, prompts for the provider: I do it myself, I hire someone else, or I am an agent. Each path has different expenses and revenue ownership.
  3. For an offline service, account for consumables, premises, labour and maximum services per month: demand cannot exceed actual capacity without adding resources. For an online service, also inspect the marketing funnel and who delivers the work. Available fields depend on the selected mode.
  4. If you act as an agent, separate the customer's payment to the principal from your commission income and specify who pays each expense. In the simplified route, I have a complex incentive scheme opens the custom-model contact screen; in the full route it proceeds to the agent model. Neither provides a general calculator for arbitrary incentive contracts. See H02 and H08.

Two distinct ways to use V2

Linked V2 starts from calculated V1 items, carrying over their inputs, price limits and applicable terms. You then add shared resources such as rent, employees, a warehouse or campaigns once for the portfolio. Do not count costs already included in V1 again. See H05.

Standalone V2 opens from I already sell several different things on the home screen. Build the assortment from scratch: items, sales-unit mix, common advertising, resources and funding. It does not import the V1 draft. Multiple goods in one V1 order are also not the same as a portfolio with shared business costs.

What to do after choosing a route

First calculate direct costs and the price required by your economics; then assess company-wide expenses and cash separately. Only then ask: who will buy from you, why, and where, at that calculated price? Which value, evidence and product presentation would persuade them? Test those assumptions with buyers and recalculate. The calculator does not verify demand (H17).

H02. How do I calculate the cost of a product or service?

Product (V1)

  1. Click Product. Enter the Product name. Click Calculate my product.
  2. In Step 2, under Where do you source it?, choose I make it, I buy it from others or Dropshipping.
  3. Pick the Sales unit (pcs, kg, m, hour and others). Quantity and price use this unit.
  4. The number of sections depends on the sourcing model: five for own production and four for resale or dropshipping, where the production section is absent. Work through the visible sections and recalculate after changing their inputs.
SectionWhat you enter
1. Cost — What makes up the cost?I make it: choose I know the price and type Material cost per unit, or choose Calculate it and list each material with + Add material. Say whether VAT is included, set the Material VAT rate, % (enter 0 if none) and inbound delivery. For imports, set who pays brokerage, cargo insurance, duties and certification: Me or Seller. Enter the Quantity in the batch.
I buy it from others: Finished units in minimum batch and Minimum batch cost. The calculator shows Purchase cost per unit. Add more products with + Add another product to the order.

Do not enter the same cost twice. Premises, staff, certificates and campaigns that serve several products belong in V2 as shared resources (H05), not in each product.

V1 · I make it — costing your own production

  1. Under What makes up the cost?, decide whether you already know the materials cost per unit. I know the price uses one amount; Calculate it lets you itemize materials with + Add material. Count consumption per one sellable unit and check the sales unit so you do not mix pieces, kilograms and metres.
  2. Say whether the purchase price includes VAT and inbound delivery. For imported materials, indicate who pays brokerage, cargo insurance, duties and documentation. Add an amount as your cost only when your business actually bears it; otherwise delivery or duty may be double-counted. See taxes and currencies.
  3. Under Production, enter premises, equipment, your own work or paid employees, utilities and overheads. Specify monthly production volume and the defect rate. Recurring costs must be spread over the applicable output, including losses, rather than charged in full to every unit.
  4. Continue through Logistics and warehouse, Sales expenses and Advertising expenses. Separate per-unit costs, percentage commissions and monthly payments. Materials or factory cost alone is not the total cost of making a sale.

V1 · I buy it from others — reselling finished goods

  1. Select I buy it from others. In Minimum batch, enter Finished units in minimum batch and Minimum batch cost. The Purchase cost per unit result is the purchasing component of a unit's cost.
  2. Illustrative input arithmetic: buying 10 units for 600 currency units means 60 per unit, before warehouse, shipping, selling fees or advertising. This is not a prediction of the final calculated selling price.
  3. For different products in the same purchase order, select + Add another product to the order. Each item keeps its own quantity and purchase cost; unlike goods are not automatically averaged together.
  4. The Production section is hidden for resale. Fill logistics and warehouse costs, selling expenses and then advertising. A batch purchase can use cash well before the whole batch is sold; H06 distinguishes cash paid for inventory from the cost of items actually sold.

V1 · Dropshipping — the supplier fulfils each order

  1. Select Dropshipping. The purchase step becomes Where and how much will I pay for the product?. Enter the Units per supplier order and the Supplier order price; add different products to the assortment separately. Supplier purchasing is not your own manufacturing cost.
  2. Under delivery terms, enter Delivery time, days and specify when the customer's funds become available to pay the supplier: after delivery or before your supplier payment. Enter an additional payout delay if relevant. These assumptions affect working capital even when the business holds no inventory.
  3. This route hides your own production and stock-storage sections. Do not attribute such expenses to the scenario unless you actually incur them elsewhere. You still need buyer delivery, selling commissions and advertising when your business pays for them.
  4. Distinguish delivery time from the moment you can use customer funds. A quick order does not guarantee funds are available before the supplier must be paid. Review cash flow and funding.

Unit costs versus costs of the entire business

Purchase and materials costs are only a starting point. Buying a batch, paying monthly rent, wages, revenue-based commissions and an advertising budget each have different timing and allocation rules. Do not apply all of a monthly rent bill to each unit. When several items share premises, employees or advertising, use V2 to record a shared resource once.

Service (V1)

  1. Click A service. Under How to model your cash flow? choose Simplified calculation (for microbusinesses) or Full calculation (VAT, taxes, credit and cash flow).
  2. Under I work: choose Online or Offline.
  3. For online services, choose a provider: I do it myself, I hire someone else or I am an agent. Full offline service calculations also offer the provider choice; simplified offline calculations open the expense form directly.
  4. For an offline service, also enter Maximum services per month. It caps how many clients you can serve.

Agents. In simplified online mode, under How do I earn? choose I get a percentage of sales and enter your commission percentage. In the full agent calculation, separately enter the Principal service price, incl. VAT, My fee, % of principal price and/or Fixed fee per service. For every nonzero expense, select Me or Principal as the payer. Your revenue is your agent remuneration, not the principal's total sales. The I have a complex incentive scheme option is not a universal incentive-contract calculator.

V1 · Offline service — consumables, capacity and premises

  1. A service → Simplified calculation → Offline opens the service-name and cost form directly. In Full calculation → Offline, first choose I do it myself, I hire someone else or I am an agent. This is still one V1 service, not V2.
  2. The unit is one service performed, such as one appointment. Enter consumables per appointment as a total or individual materials. Record premises and equipment rental, employees, utilities and other actual service-delivery costs.
  3. Enter Maximum services per month as the real limit imposed by time, staff and workstations. The sales forecast comes from the advertising funnel, not from manually declaring an unlimited number of manufactured units. If the scenario exceeds capacity, revise the resources or assumptions.
  4. Do not invent warehousing or manufacturing defects for a service: goods-only sections are hidden. Complete selling and advertising expenses and, in full mode, the tax and cash schedule.

V1 · Simplified online service — solo or hired provider

  1. Choose A service → Simplified calculation → Online and then I do it myself or I hire someone else. The single-service form models a marketing funnel and a selected time period.
  2. Enter the monthly advertising budget, CPC, CTR and click-to-lead and lead-to-deal conversions. Consultation price, consultation package price and the share purchasing a package are used to model sales revenue. They are not automatically the provider's costs of delivering the service.
  3. Set the calculation period in months, campaign management, website, hosting, domain, lead magnet, card-acquiring fee and turnover tax where applicable. The I hire someone else branch also includes Markup on contractor cost, which separates payment to the contractor from your business result.
  4. Consultations and package sales are based on your assumed funnel conversion rates, not verified orders. Look at the period totals and monthly breakdown instead of treating predicted leads as actual buyers. See H04.

V1 · Agent — two businesses involved in one sale

  1. In Simplified calculation → Online → I am an agent select I get a percentage of sales. Enter My fee as a share of sales and your own turnover tax separately. The principal's customer sales do not become your revenue: your revenue is the commission.
  2. For each nonzero expense, choose Who pays: Me / Principal. For example, if you pay for advertising, that cost reduces your net commission; if the principal pays, it belongs to the principal's business. The two parties' figures are presented separately.
  3. In the full agent path, use the different detailed inputs: Principal service price including VAT, My fee, % of principal price, Fixed fee per service and expense-payer selections. Check each party's taxes, including the sales tax regime.
  4. In simplified mode I have a complex incentive scheme offers contact for a bespoke model; in full mode it continues to the existing agent calculation. Neither is a universal calculator for arbitrary multi-tier commission agreements.

V1 · Full calculation of one service: what changes

Full does not mean the multi-item V2. For online services, first complete the original marketing, delivery and expense inputs, give the service a name and select Calculate complete service finances →. For offline services, complete V1's unit costs, sales assumptions, advertising, capacity and price before opening its full cash model. The next step adds the applicable tax settings, planning horizon, owner funds or credit, and reserve; it reports period-by-period expenses and cash. Validation can prevent the full result until the source V1 calculation is complete.

Check your inputs before calculating a price

Verify the unit to which each cost applies, who pays it, whether it is one-off, per-unit or monthly, whether tax and shipping are already included, and whether forecast orders exceed capacity or realistic delivery terms. Then read the calculated price and margin in H03 and the timing of money in H06. You must verify supplier quotes, conversion assumptions and tax rates yourself.

H03. How do I set a selling price and target margin?

In product V1, you first enter costs, marketing-funnel assumptions, taxes, funding and your target margin; the calculator then derives the required selling price. It answers “What would I need to charge for this product under these assumptions?”—not how much customers will actually pay. Some online-service and agency paths instead ask you to enter consultation prices or contractual remuneration directly. The V1 product price-list range is explained below.

In V1

  1. Finish the cost sections, advertising, Portfolio VAT and Business tax (On revenue or On profit, plus Tax rate, %). If no business tax is selected, you will see: “Select revenue tax or profit tax above to calculate a numeric price.”
  2. Enter the target product margin for each product. Under Will you offer discounts? select Yes or No. For “Yes”, specify Maximum discount, %; “No” makes it 0%. Enter Minimum margin, % separately. The minimum must not exceed the target.
  3. Read the price block, titled “final price including loan and taxes”: Product price, VAT and Final customer price.
  4. Read the Price-list range. Both endpoints are list prices before a discount: the lower endpoint is designed to preserve your minimum margin after the maximum discount; the upper preserves your target margin after that same maximum discount. These are not the post-discount amounts, and neither should be confused with the single Final customer price.

If the calculator reports that no profitable price is possible at these tax, commission and margin rates, reduce costs or lower the target margin.

What goes into the required V1 price?

  1. Start with the economics of one unit. V1 uses unit cost and logistics, the cost of acquiring an order (CAC) from the advertising funnel, allocated loan interest and a per-unit reserve. The target margin is initially applied before the business-tax adjustment.
  2. Business costs and taxes matter too. The final calculation takes account of variable selling fees and deductions, shared operating costs, the selected turnover or profit tax and VAT if applicable. VAT is not the same as profit tax and is shown separately. The amount charged to the buyer can therefore differ from the preliminary pre-tax price.
  3. A customer price and a price-list range are different results. The “final price including loan and taxes” block displays one calculated price and its VAT treatment. Below, the list-price range is recalculated against two margin levels and the maximum discount. Do not automatically substitute the upper list-price endpoint for the actual V1 customer price in other calculations.
  4. Forecast sales affect the price. CAC depends on your budget, CPC and assumed conversion rates; shared monthly operating costs are spread over forecast sold units. Changing conversion or sales volume changes the economics, so this is not a permanent intrinsic price of the product.

How discounts affect both price-list endpoints

The calculation produces a price before the maximum discount. To keep the chosen margin when that discount is applied, the displayed list price must allow for it. Therefore the discount enters both the minimum-margin and target-margin endpoint calculations. Answering No sets the discount to 0%, while minimum and target margins still remain separate goals.

Simplified example: suppose the complete relevant unit cost is 100, with no commissions, additional taxes or VAT; minimum margin 10%, target margin 20%, maximum discount 10%. The required pre-discount list-price range is 123.46–138.89. After applying the full 10% discount, the buyer would pay approximately 111.11–125.00, preserving the respective margins. This is an illustration with omitted real-world costs, not a live V1 result.

Why the range may be unavailable

  1. You must complete the unit-cost, advertising and sales assumptions. The model needs positive expected orders and a defined CAC. Resale and dropshipping also need a valid supplier purchase amount; missing or invalid inputs prevent a numeric range.
  2. Select Business tax—On revenue or On profit—and specify whether VAT applies, including a VAT rate when required. Product V1 currently uses one VAT rate for the calculated product set.
  3. For the range calculation, minimum and target margins must both be strictly above 0% and below 100%, with the minimum no higher than the target. Maximum discount must be from 0% up to but not including 100%. “No” sets the discount to zero. If selling fees, taxes and requested margin make the equation impossible, the calculator shows an error instead of an invented price.
  4. An absent result is not a zero selling price. Read the message beneath the range, correct the inputs and recalculate. Also review the currency, unit of sale and allocated shared expenses (H02, H08).

Margin is not markup

Example (illustrative numbers)

Cost 100, price 125 before tax.

Margin = (125 − 100) ÷ 125 = 20%. Markup = (125 − 100) ÷ 100 = 25%.

The calculator's margin fields are margins on price. Markup divides by cost.

Using your calculated price to test the business idea

Next ask who would buy this product, why, and through which channel at this price. What product features would justify it, and what value proposition would make it credible? If the calculated price seems higher than prospective buyers might accept, do not simply reduce your margin or raise conversion assumptions to force an attractive result. Revisit sourcing, product configuration, costs, customer segment, acquisition channels and positioning. V1 tests the internal commercial coherence of your assumptions; market demand and willingness to pay still require validation. See H17.

Full-service agent exception: the agent's contractual earnings depend on the principal's price, a commission percentage and/or a fixed fee. In that route, both displayed price-range endpoints may be equal; this is not the standard two-margin product price solver. Simplified online services likewise use your entered consultation and package prices (H02).

In V2

  • Standalone V2: each product has Target product margin, %. The base price comes from that target and the base CAC. The actual margin after weighted CAC can differ, and the result shows Weighted product profit margin, %.
  • Linked V2: the V1 price range stays fixed. V2 shows the Calculated V2 list price, which cannot exceed the approved V1 ceiling. The status text tells you whether the target or minimum margin is reached.

A product's margin before shared costs is not the profit of the whole business. See H05 and H06. More: product and service pricing calculator.

H04. What do CAC, CPC, CTR, conversions and ROAS mean?

In product V1's Advertising expenses, in service V1 marketing and in standalone V2's 1. Advertising and marketing, you enter an acquisition hypothesis: budget, cost per click and conversions. The calculator derives a scenario forecast of inquiries and purchases; it does not measure real campaigns. Despite similar labels, CAC and financial results include different costs across the V1 paths.

FieldMeans
Monthly advertising budgetWhat you plan to spend on ads per month.
Average cost per click (CPC)Budget ÷ CPC = clicks.
CTR — ad click-through rate, %Clicks as a share of impressions. Used to show impressions.
Conversion: click → lead / inquiry, %Share of clicks that become leads.
Conversion: lead / inquiry → purchase, %Share of leads that buy.
Marketing manager fee / monthEnter 0 if there is none.

Example (illustrative numbers)

  • Budget 1,000, CPC 2 → 500 clicks.
  • 10% click → lead: 50 leads.
  • 20% lead → purchase: 10 purchases.
  • Base CAC = 1,000 ÷ 10 = 100.
  • With 5% CTR, 500 clicks mean 10,000 impressions.

What you see in product V1 and the full offline/online service cost form. The advertising block shows impressions, clicks, leads, purchases and modeled sales units. Net advertising budget per unit = ad budget ÷ forecast purchases; this is the item's Base CAC. Marketing fee per unit = monthly fee ÷ purchases. Total customer acquisition cost per unit in this particular block is their sum, not the cost of making the product or delivering the service. Management fees are accounted for separately from Base CAC in the wider business model.

V1 · Product: build the funnel step by step

  1. After costs, logistics and selling expenses, open Advertising expenses. Enter the monthly ad budget and average CPC for each item. If the budget is positive, CPC must also be positive; otherwise the model cannot derive meaningful clicks or Base CAC.
  2. Enter the ad click-through rate, CTR %. This calculator derives clicks from budget and CPC, then uses CTR to work backward to the corresponding impression count. Raising CTR alone while keeping spend and CPC constant does not create extra purchases in this model.
  3. Enter the two conversions separately: click → lead/inquiry and lead/inquiry → purchase. A lead is not a customer. The forecast passes from spend to clicks, then inquiries and finally purchases, after which the advertising cost of a purchase can be computed.
  4. Enter the marketing management fee per month if paid separately. Do not count that fee again as ad platform spend. Select Recalculate, then compare the media cost, management cost and their per-unit sum.
  5. In product V1, the advertising block sets units sold per month equal to forecast purchases. This assumes one sellable unit per purchase within this funnel, not a measured average basket size.

If forecast purchases are zero: the block may display zero per-unit advertising cost because it cannot divide by zero. That does not mean acquiring a customer is free: the product pricing model treats Base CAC as undefined, so a valid selling-price calculation cannot be completed. Recheck CPC, both conversions and budget.

Example with a marketing management fee

Extend the illustrative funnel above with a monthly management fee of 200. With a 1,000 advertising budget and 10 modeled purchases:

  • Base CAC: 1,000 ÷ 10 = 100 per purchase.
  • Management: 200 ÷ 10 = 20 per purchase.
  • “Total customer acquisition cost per unit” in the advertising block: 100 + 20 = 120.

This is not the total manufacturing cost, business profit or final customer price. Delivery, sales fees, tax and funding are accounted for separately.

Standalone V2 adds a CAC buffer ratio, % (100% means no buffer; 75% means CAC ÷ 0.75), a Minimum period, months, and a weighted CAC that spreads the shared budget across products. The result also shows Business ROAS, as revenue ÷ ad spend.

Simplified online service V1 outside agent mode displays CPA / CAC and ROMI cards. CPA here is advertising spend over the selected period ÷ modeled deals in the monthly scenario. CAC is operating expenses plus amortization for the same period ÷ deals: it may include advertising, contractor payments, payment-processing fees, campaign management and other recognized expenses. Turnover tax is not included in this CAC numerator. With additional costs, CAC is generally higher than CPA, but they may be equal. In online agent mode, the headline cards instead show what the agent keeps, the principal's profit and the principal's cash flow; the principal's CPA/CAC appear in the detailed report, not in the ordinary card.

V1 · Simplified online service: what changes?

  1. Service → Simplified calculation → Online opens a different set of inputs: Monthly advertising budget, Average CPC, CTR, Click → Lead conversion and Lead → Deal conversion. Set the calculation period in months; total advertising spend for the period is the monthly budget multiplied by that period.
  2. The form also asks for consultation price, consultation package price and the share of customers who buy a package. These are sales-revenue assumptions, not media costs. The funnel gives an expected number of deals; package sales also depend on the package uptake assumption.
  3. The monthly breakdown uses a particular launch scenario: deals are reduced in month one, with additional reductions to package sales and recognized revenue. Therefore read the displayed period CPA/CAC rather than mechanically multiplying a full-month funnel result by the number of months and assuming identical timing.
  4. Other expenses include marketing management, site, hosting, domain, lead magnet, acquiring fees and taxes. Hiring a contractor adds contractor expense. In agent mode, specify which nonzero costs are paid by the agent and which by the principal (H02).

CPA, CAC, ROAS and ROMI are not interchangeable

  • CPA in simplified online services: advertising spend for the period divided by modeled deals. This is ad cost per deal, not the cost of operating the whole business.
  • CAC in the same form: operating expenses plus recognized amortization for the period divided by modeled deals. This differs from product Base CAC, whose numerator is only media spend. In agent mode the reported full CAC belongs to the principal, not solely to the agent.
  • ROAS: a ratio of revenue to advertising spend. Simplified online V1 uses revenue after acquiring fees in its ROAS result; product summaries or V2 may label revenue differently. Read the actual report label rather than applying one numerator definition to every route.
  • ROMI in an ordinary simplified online service: marketing return in percent. The current calculation deducts contractor delivery, acquiring fees and turnover tax from modeled revenue, then deducts the ad budget and management cost; it divides that contribution by ad and management spend. Website, lead magnet and other investment costs are tracked separately. ROMI is not net profit or cash flow.

Offline and full service V1: check capacity and who pays

For offline services and full online services, the funnel appears inside the goods-style V1 cost form and supplies the expected number of paid services. For offline services, compare this forecast with Maximum services per month: a forecast exceeding physical capacity cannot validate the price without changed resources or assumptions. In full-service agent mode, the agent or principal may pay advertising costs, while the principal's gross customer receipts never become the agent's revenue. Do not confuse this full V1 service form with the simplified online CPA/CAC cards.

Audit the acquisition assumptions before accepting a price

For each figure, ask where it came from: CPC for which platform and geography, conversion rates for which audience and offering, deal volume relative to capacity or supplier lead time, and budget funded by whom. Try a higher CPC and lower conversions to test the sensitivity of CAC, required price and cash commitments. These are commercial feasibility scenarios, not evidence that sales will occur.

Using the outcome. The forecast cost of winning a sale is part of product economics and can change the price you need to charge (H03). But the funnel remains hypothetical: to justify the modeled sales, test who would buy this product or service, why, through which channel and for which value at that price. Lead volume alone does not prove demand (H17).

H05. How are shared expenses allocated in V2?

A portfolio brings separate saleable items (SKUs) into one business model. An SKU is an individual product or service with its own unit cost, demand forecast, advertising burden and price. A shared expense is one actual payment serving several SKUs, such as premises, staff, certification or a website. Enter it once and identify the beneficiaries: allocation explains how one payment is attributed, not additional invoices.

Two V2 routes: where a portfolio starts

  • Standalone V2: start from the home-screen route I already sell several different things. Define the product assortment, unit costs, unit-based sales mix, shared funnel and resources from scratch. This does not import your personal V1 draft.
  • Linked V2: start with calculated goods or services from V1. Their canonical IDs, approved price-list bounds, unit costs, sales forecasts, ad budgets, CAC and tax terms come across. You do not re-enter these inputs: V2 assembles a portfolio and adds common resources. Your original V1 calculation is not rewritten.
  • Both routes produce portfolio economics but have different sources. Standalone V2 is not an extension of V1 and builds a product assortment; linked V2 can combine eligible goods and services from V1. Purchasing several units of one SKU does not itself create multiple SKUs.

Standalone V2 — nine sections

  1. Advertising and marketing: one portfolio funnel (H04).
  2. Products and sales mix: for each product choose I make it, I buy it from others or Dropshipping, then enter its cost, Target product margin, % and Forecast sales mix by UNITS, %. All products together must equal 100%. Use + Add product.
  3. Procurement and certificates.
  4. Production: premises, staff, equipment.
  5. Warehouse and logistics.
  6. Sales and team.
  7. Advertising budgets and marketing staff: add only separate campaigns and fees. The shared budget already follows each product's revenue share.
  8. Other business expenses: website, domain, content and so on.
  9. Taxes, owner funds and credit (H07, H08).

SKU, unit sales mix and monetary revenue share are not the same

Standalone V2 has a card for each product. Enter the name, supply method (I make it, I buy it from others, Dropshipping), unit cost, target margin and Forecast sales mix by UNITS, %. This is each item's share of the projected order count from the common funnel. All SKU shares must total exactly 100%; entering 100% for every product is invalid.

The monetary share is calculated from forecast revenue per SKU: calculated selling price × forecast sales. Standalone V2 uses this revenue-based weighting to allocate the base shared ad budget and determine each SKU's weighted CAC. Its initial price uses common acquisition cost with the chosen CAC buffer; the realized margin after allocation can differ from the entered target. A cost or campaign already assigned to one SKU must not be duplicated as a new common invoice.

Example: unit mix differs from revenue share (illustrative only).

Out of 100 forecast purchases, product A sells 75 units at 100 and product B sells 25 units at 300. The unit split is 75% / 25%, but forecast revenue is 7,500 each, giving a 50% / 50% monetary split. One monthly rental payment of 600 allocated By sales revenue assigns 300 to each SKU, while the business pays only 600. With manual usage in a 3:1 ratio, that same payment would be assigned as 450 and 150. This demonstrates the allocation rule, not a live portfolio result.

Adding a shared resource

In standalone V2, use sections 3 to 8. In linked V2, use 2. Shared resources and click + Add shared resource.

  1. Pick the Resource type: Premises, Workers, Warehouse rent, Equipment, Certificate / license, Sales staff, Advertising management, Separate advertising campaign, Website, Hosting, Domain, Content or Other shared expenses.
  2. Enter Amount and set Payment to Monthly or One time.
  3. Under Resource supports products, select every product it serves. One payment is shared by several products. Do not charge it twice.
  4. Under How to allocate across products, choose By sales revenue or By actual utilization. For utilization, enter each product's usage in one unit (hours, days or a proportion) and, if there is a limit, the Total resource capacity.
  5. A certificate can cover several products only if its country, product scope and validity cover all of them.

How one shared payment is allocated

  1. Beneficiaries: select only the SKUs using the resource. An empty beneficiary list is not permission to allocate silently to every product; correct the selection first.
  2. By sales revenue: apportion the amount by forecast revenue among the selected SKUs only. In linked V2 this calculation reflects the selected discount and excludes VAT from the monetary base. The shares can change as prices, volumes or ad burdens change.
  3. By actual utilization: enter comparable usage for each beneficiary, such as equipment-hours. Linked V2 additionally offers Per sale / visit unit for premises, workers, equipment and sales staff: resource use per unit is multiplied by forecast sales. Exceeding a declared capacity invalidates the scenario.
  4. Monthly vs one time: recurring expenses apply to operating periods; one-time expenses are paid once. Do not relabel an expense just to improve the result: frequency affects P&L, funding and cash flow. An extra campaign is a monthly advertising expense allocated by the revenue shares of its beneficiaries.
  5. Conservation: the allocated lines for each resource must sum to the single amount entered. Allocation changes which SKU carries the cost, not how much the business actually pays.

Linked V2: avoid counting V1 costs twice

  1. Under 2. Shared resources, add the real shared payment, identify its SKUs and set What should be replaced in the old calculations?
  2. Use New expense (not included in V1) when it really is new: nothing is deducted from the old SKU costs.
  3. If the payment was already embedded in V1, choose the correct source: Already in SKU cost, Already in SKU selling expenses, Already in SKU marketing manager fee, or, for a common campaign, Already in SKU advertising budget. Enter the Amount already in V1, per month separately for each affected SKU. This is the amount being replaced, not the new common resource amount.
  4. When replacing embedded unit cost, also identify its original cash payment (materials/procurement, production or fulfillment/delivery). Mixed full-service portfolios can specify source categories per SKU; these distinctions preserve the timing and accounting of cash payments.
  5. V2 rejects offsets larger than their source expense. You cannot deduct a one-off resource from a monthly V1 charge without an amortization schedule. The new shared payment is added once and the confirmed old portion removed from source lines. If the replacement amount stays at zero, the V1 amount remains and the new invoice is added on top.

Example (illustrative numbers)

Rent of 300 a month serves two products. The business pays 300, not 600. The shares given to the two products must add up to 300.

The result table has an Allocated to column. It shows which products each shared cost was assigned to. Warehouse costs cannot be assigned to offline or online services: a service is not warehouse inventory.

Price and forecast rules when moving V1 → linked V2

Under 1. V1 product and service economics, each card shows original unit cost, monthly forecast, ad budget, CAC and V1 price range. Full services with a fixed V1 quote or contractual agent fee cannot be freely repriced as ordinary goods: V2 keeps the contractual pricing result. Offline services are checked against monthly service capacity; physical goods against available stock, production and supply lead times. Mixed portfolios must also keep tax regime and currency compatible rather than quietly combining conflicting inputs.

Period, inventory, credit and saved drafts

A one-month summary differs from multi-month cash flow for the full operating cycle. Where V1 provides an approved period, linked V2 carries forward physical stock sell-through and the service operating horizon; a longer loan repayment term never creates extra sales. Where future demand duration is unverified, specify the months and approve the recurring-demand assumption before forecasting further periods. Procurement prepayments are cash payments while COGS is recognized when stock sells; do not add the original advance to cost of goods again (H08).

Standalone and linked V2 keep separate saved drafts. Bringing another calculated SKU from V1 preserves shared settings for overlapping IDs, but does not automatically make a new SKU a beneficiary of existing resources. Review the assignments after import. Removing a SKU from linked V2 recalculates that portfolio and leaves the original V1 draft untouched.

H06. Why is profit different from cash flow and Available Cash?

Profit describes the financial result of goods sold or services delivered during a period, after the expenses recognized for that period. Cash flow shows when money actually arrives and leaves: customers pay, suppliers demand advances, staff and advertising are paid, and loans fall due. A profitable business can lack the cash needed for its next obligation.

Revenue, COGS, EBITDA, profit and cash are different layers

  1. Revenue: the value of sales completed or assumed by the scenario. It is not profit, and the date of a sale does not necessarily match the date cash is collected.
  2. Cost of goods sold (COGS): the cost of units actually sold. Unsold stock is not expensed as if it were already sold, although paying an advance for that stock requires cash immediately.
  3. EBITDA: an intermediate operating result before interest, profit taxes and depreciation/amortization where the model separates them. Do not treat a particular screen's EBITDA as a bank balance or a universal accounting figure: the precise composition follows the selected model.
  4. Net profit: the result after the applicable costs and tax in that scenario. Check the reporting period and tax regime. In standalone V2, Net profit · baseline month is calculated before loan interest; loan interest is handled separately in the monthly schedule.
  5. Cash flow: receipts less payments on their actual timeline, including procurement advances, advertising, one-off expenses, interest and principal repayments. Its cumulative figure can differ from profit even if the sales total is identical.

Example: positive gross profit but money tied up in stock

Illustrative example: one supplier payment and two sales only; no tax, advertising, payment fees, rent, interest or other costs.

  • Month 1: buy 10 units at 60 each, paying the supplier 600 before any sales. Cash change: −600.
  • Month 2: sell 2 units at 100 each. Revenue 200; COGS 2 × 60 = 120; gross profit = 80.
  • Cumulative change in cash across the two months: −600 + 200 = −400. Eight units remain in stock; 480 of the payment relates to those unsold units. Positive gross profit in month two does not eliminate the cash requirement.

Customer payment delays or another required inventory purchase can make the gap larger. The example does not claim net profit of 80: the other expenses were deliberately omitted.

The payment calendar: what is paid, and when?

In standalone V2, an initial stock advance is paid at the start while unit COGS is recognized as units sell. Storage charges depend on unsold inventory and time held. Dropshipping has no owned opening stock batch; supplier settlement is associated with orders. Shared resources follow their declared payment frequency, without multiplying one invoice by the number of SKUs (H05).

Linked V2 may additionally reflect procurement and production lead times, settlement after delivery in dropshipping, different inventory sell-through dates, service operating horizons and loan repayment schedules. Identical monthly prices and demand can therefore produce very different cash balances. For multi-month models, check which dates and recurring-demand assumptions have been established in V1.

“AVAILABLE CASH”: read the definition for your actual route

  • Standalone V2: AVAILABLE CASH = cumulative cash flow − protected reserve. Cumulative cash flow includes owner money or a loan received, modeled sales and payments, interest and principal repayment. This is a modeled spendable balance after the reserve, not net income or free cash flow under an accounting standard.
  • Linked V2: the result likewise distinguishes Ending cash balance and AVAILABLE CASH after protected reserves. Required funding and reserves are recalculated using the shared resources and actual cash calendar without recharging old V1 expenses a second time.
  • Simplified online service V1: the AVAILABLE CASH card represents cumulative cash results for the selected period. In ordinary mode, that is receipts less expenses and taxes, including one-time payments, with no separate protected reserve. In agent mode it is your agent commission less expenses you pay and your own tax, not all cash belonging to the principal. Nonzero expenses need assigned payers before that agent result is confirmed (H02).
  • Simplified offline service V1: the cash calculation takes account of the service operating period, any longer receipt/payment calendar, financing, taxes and a protected reserve. Compare this figure with profit only over equivalent periods.

If AVAILABLE CASH is negative, the modeled ending balance does not cover its protected reserve; the bank account need not itself be below zero. A negative cumulative cash balance indicates a negative overall cash position in the scenario.

Startup funding, reserve and loan payments

  1. Startup funds: standalone V2 derives the required amount from the maximum forecast liquidity shortfall and unavoidable early payments, not from summing every projected expense twice. A reserve is added to that funding need. Funds injected by the owner or received from a lender bridge that shortfall, but do not create profit.
  2. Protected reserve: a portion of funding held back when reporting spendable cash. It is not another supplier payment or a second charge for rent, stock or ads.
  3. Interest: a cash expense under the debt schedule that reduces available cash; check the selected model and period to see how it enters profit.
  4. Loan principal: a cash repayment to the lender, not an additional COGS charge or an operating loss. In standalone V2's current baseline debt schedule, interest is paid monthly and principal is repaid in the last loan month.

Reading standalone V2's month-by-month result

Follow Month → Sales → Payments → Operating cash flow → Loan / owner → Interest → Principal repayment → Net CF → Cumulative CF → AVAILABLE CASH. Net CF includes financing and debt payments; it may jump in the month a loan arrives and fall when its principal is repaid. Cumulative CF adds the monthly changes from the beginning of the scenario. The model uses a 30-day month.

EBITDA · baseline month and Net profit · baseline month describe a base-month economic view, not the cash balance at the end of the whole cycle. The modeled cycle outcome separately reflects interest charges. Do not directly compare one month's profit with a cash balance after several months of sales and debt payments.

Testing commercial resilience

Review more than profit: track the first supplier payment, advertising before receipts, customer collection timing, inventory exhaustion, loan maturity and lowest cash balance. If profit is positive but cash is insufficient, inspect advances, settlement terms, financing and batch size rather than mechanically raising the price. Conversely, borrowed capital and positive AVAILABLE CASH do not establish profitability or demand. First estimate the economically required price (H03), then ask who will buy, why and through which channel at that price (H17).

H07. How do I compare my own funds with a business loan?

The calculator separates how much cash the business needs before receipts arrive, where that money comes from, and what the financing costs. Owner contributions and borrowings provide liquidity, but are not profit. A protected reserve is cash held back from spending; it is not a second business expense or an automatic tax deduction (H06).

Where financing is entered in each calculation route

  • Standalone V2, section “9. Taxes, owner funds and credit”: set Capital source → Own funds or Loan, then Protected reserve, % of cash deficit. For a loan enter the Annual credit rate, % and Loan term, months. The funding amount is derived from the portfolio's cash-flow model; it is not an arbitrary loan request.
  • V1 goods: under Calculate a loan, review Loan principal, Annual loan rate, %, Loan term, months and the separate Reserve, % of planned expenses. The principal comes from the computed capital requirement for the applicable sourcing or production cycle.
  • V1 full service: choose Funding → Own funds / Loan and enter Reserve, Annual loan rate, % and Loan term, months. Results include Startup capital, Peak deficit, First positive CF, Payback and, when applicable, Outstanding debt.
  • Linked V2: imports approved cash and debt terms from the calculated V1 SKUs, then recomputes shared resources, portfolio funding needs and the cash calendar. Do not add the same borrowing or reserve again as a separate common resource (H05).

Startup funding versus the protected reserve

Standalone V2: the model first finds the cash deficit before financing, including early committed payments for inventory, advertising and one-off resources that later receipts cannot finance in advance. Deficit = the maximum of the upfront payment requirement and negative cumulative cash balances. Then Reserve = Deficit × reserve percentage / 100, and Required funding = Deficit + Reserve. This is a scenario estimate, not a bank's credit approval.

V1 goods: the base amount can include the costs committed over an entire stock sell-through cycle without deducting future sales receipts. Dropshipping instead accounts for advertising paid in advance and the funding gap before buyer funds are released. Each V1 route calculates the reserve from its defined capital base, so it is not automatically comparable with standalone V2's “% of cash deficit”. V1 full service derives its reserve from first-month variable expenditure, including commissions, and fixed costs; equipment cash outlay (CAPEX) is distinct from depreciation.

Standalone V2 reserve example (illustrative figures).

If the maximum pre-funding cash deficit is 1,000 and the reserve rate is 15%, the reserve is 150 and required owner or loan funding is 1,150. The additional 150 is protected liquidity, not a second supplier bill. If cumulative closing cash is later 250, AVAILABLE CASH after reserve is 100.

How loan interest and principal repayment work

The displayed loan models use a bullet repayment, not equal monthly annuity payments: Monthly interest = principal × annual percentage rate / 100 / 12; Total interest = monthly interest × the months charged. Principal falls due at maturity, with interest paid separately. Do not assume lender fees, insurance or variable rates are included unless that particular form explicitly models them.

Loan example (illustrative figures, no extra charges).

At 1,200 principal, 12% annual interest and 6 months, interest is 12 per month and 72 in total. Under this schedule, the first five months pay interest only; month six pays 12 interest + 1,200 principal. Total repayment to the lender is 1,272. The financial plan must still show how that 1,200 principal becomes available on time.

Standalone V2 charges monthly interest on the calculated funding amount, not on the example number. Its cash schedule may continue to maturity after sales have stopped. A longer term adds interest; it does not invent additional customers. In the V1 full-service model, profit and debt balances are reported for the chosen service operating horizon. If loan maturity extends beyond it, Outstanding debt can appear rather than pretending that principal has already been repaid.

Why comparing the loan rate with gross margin is insufficient

V1 goods highlight a warning when the entered annual loan rate is not below the product's target margin percentage. But an annual interest rate and the margin on a single sale have different denominators and time horizons: comparing the two is not a stand-alone credit approval test. Compare interest over the full term, profit from the entire sales forecast, financing cost per unit, stock sell-through time and capacity to repay principal. A rate below margin can still coincide with a cash shortfall; the warning is a prompt to examine the model, not a substitute for it.

V1 goods include the modeled financing-interest burden when testing the required selling price over forecast sales volume, rather than treating borrowing as free. Linked V2 respects the original V1 price boundaries; pooled costs and financing do not authorize a made-up higher price beyond the approved range (H03, H05).

Reading the results without mistaking funding for income

  1. Startup funds / Startup capital: funding required to cover the modeled liquidity need. Owner equity and received loan proceeds are financing, not sales revenue.
  2. Peak deficit: the worst pre-funding shortage on the relevant cash timeline. It can occur as soon as a supplier advance is due, before the first customer pays, not necessarily at month end.
  3. First positive CF: the first period with a positive operating flow; it does not prove that the initial investment has been recovered.
  4. Payback: the separate test of accumulated operating recovery over the modeled horizon; it is not automatically the first profitable or cash-positive month.
  5. Cumulative CF / Ending cash balance: cash movements including funding, interest and principal repayment. In V2, AVAILABLE CASH excludes the protected reserve and is not net profit (H06).
  6. Outstanding debt: a loan balance that remains beyond the shown operating horizon. It is not disposable owner cash.

Business decision: owner capital or borrowing?

Compare two cases using the same prices, products, demand, tax assumptions and operating costs, changing only the capital source, rate and term. Check required price including financing, startup funding, total cycle interest, minimum spendable balance, profit and the principal repayment date. Owner funds avoid interest but remain tied up in the venture. A loan preserves some owner liquidity but creates a binding repayment schedule. Neither validates market demand: after computing the price, test who will buy, why, and where at that price (H17).

You can use the cotton tote example to compare No loan · 5 months with With loan · 6 months (sample). These are model scenarios, not a lender offer.

H08. How do I choose between VAT, Sales Tax and no sales tax?

Two separate settings:

  1. Tax mode (tax on sales): VAT / IVA, Sales tax or No sales tax. For VAT you enter the rate. For Sales Tax you enter Sales Tax rate, %.
  2. Business tax: On revenue or On profit, with Tax rate, %. VAT is separate and not an alternative to this tax.

You type every rate yourself. The calculator does not know your country's rates.

Sales Tax is added to the price after the discount. It passes through to the state and is not your revenue. The V2 results show revenue without tax, the Sales Tax total and the total the buyers pay.
